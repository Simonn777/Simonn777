PolyTSL

Testing whether a small adapter can connect text descriptions to a frozen polymer generator.

ROLE
Undergraduate researcher; first-author manuscript draft
TOOLS
Text+ChemT5 · MolGPT · adapter training
CONTEXT
NSF-funded REU · Jun–Aug 2026

The approach

Frozen Text+ChemT5→593K adapter→Frozen MolGPT

I developed a cross-attention adapter with 592,768 trainable parameters between a text encoder and a polymer generator. Holding the larger models fixed isolates the behavior of the learned bridge.

The output representation is PSMILES. The research question is about text conditioning, not just whether generated strings are valid.

Evaluation design

  • Dataset: 13,020 description–structure pairs across 735 polymer graphs.
  • Split: group descriptions by polymer graph before assigning training, validation, and test sets.
  • Controlled benchmark: 89 graph-disjoint targets evaluated across five seeds.
  • Baselines: a parameter-matched pooled MLP and retrieval approaches.
  • Ablations: shuffled, mismatched and neutral descriptions, plus a gate-off control.

Several descriptions can refer to the same graph. Splitting descriptions independently can place the same target structure on both sides of a split, which changes what the evaluation measures.

What I can conclude

The controlled results support text-sensitive conditioning relative to no-text and perturbation controls. They do not establish a statistically significant advantage over the pooled MLP, exact repeat-unit recovery, or state-of-the-art performance. Retrieval remains an important baseline.

That distinction matters: responding to the text is a weaker claim than recovering the correct structure.

First-author manuscript draft, not a peer-reviewed publication. Source, data, model assets, and manuscript files remain private pending release review.
