Different rows do not necessarily mean different entities.

Consider graph G01 with two descriptions. A row split can assign description A to training and description B to testing. The text is different, but G01 is already represented in training. A graph split keeps A and B together.

The right split depends on the question. Row-level splitting can be appropriate when the target is new descriptions of known entities. To test generalization to unseen graphs, the graph identity is the relevant split unit.

Zero overlapping graph IDs checks this split boundary only. It does not establish model quality, rule out overlap in upstream pretraining, or detect equivalent real graphs assigned different IDs. The toy graphs here have distinct node counts; no graph-isomorphism method is being evaluated.

The demo uses equal group sizes, so both strategies have identical row counts. With uneven group sizes, reserving 25% of groups need not reserve exactly 25% of rows.
